The one truth that organises everything

You're not building a better Glassdoor. You're building the registry.

In Nigeria and Kenya, the dominant "technology" for answering what should I earn, is this company any good, where can this role lead, who's hiring is relational — family, old-boy networks, church, professional bodies, WhatsApp groups, "long leg." Most good jobs are never posted; they move through trusted circles first.

In Porter's language this relational network is a substitute, but it behaves like the incumbent market leader: high trust, zero monetary cost, deeply entrenched, and structurally exclusionary. The real job is to convert a trust-based oral information market into an objective, queryable one — where the underlying data largely doesn't exist yet. (Zillow without a land registry: the registry is the product; the UI is the easy part.)

🎯 Compete on trust & freshness, not features

  • The relational channel wins on trust; free aggregators win on breadth.
  • You win only if data is both trustworthy and broader than a WhatsApp group…
  • …and fresher than a static Glassdoor figure in a 30%-inflation economy.

💸 The person with the problem isn't the one with the budget

  • Job seekers expect free.
  • The money sits with employers, recruiters and institutions.
  • That split decides the business model before anything else.

The category most scans miss — and the biggest. These are the substitutes people actually use today. The relational system is free and high-trust, but it manufactures the "network gap": opportunity allocated by who you know, not what you can do (a referred candidate is ~9× more likely to be hired).

👨‍👩‍👧 The "uncle" network

  • Family / "long-leg" / "man-know-man" referral hiring
  • Named wasta regionally — ~85% say jobs come this way in one Egyptian youth survey

🎓 Alumni networks

  • Old Students' Associations, university set/year groups, course WhatsApp groups
  • Durable and status-coded

⛪ Religious networks

  • RCCG / Winners / Catholic parishes & mosque communities
  • Job and information pipelines

🏛️ Professional bodies

  • ICAN, CIPM, NBA, NSE
  • CIPM membership now near-baseline for TA roles

🤝 Ethnic / hometown unions

  • Town unions; the Igbo Igba Boi apprenticeship model
  • Relational capital as career infrastructure

💬 Word-of-mouth pay

  • People learn what to earn by quietly asking peers
  • Never by checking a site

The strategic crux: CoffeeChat's deepest reason to exist is to democratise and make objective what's locked inside private networks. The global/remote market is far more merit-based — which is why convertibility is so attractive. For many users, going global is partly an escape from wasta.

4 · Verdict

The five things that must be true

Real · Can win · Worth doing

The screen's bottom line, in three reads:

Real? Yes — but the buyer is B2B, not the consumer.
Can win? Yes on the data wedge — conditional on resourcing the gap.
Worth doing? Yes — strongest on strategic fit; remaining work is retiring execution risk.
1

B2B pays

Recruiters/TA teams + corporates/DFIs will pay for benchmarking, talent intelligence and company data — because consumers won't. Test first; it gates the model.

2

You out-trust the network and out-fresh Glassdoor

Verified, currency-honest data beats both "ask my uncle" (objectivity/breadth) and a stale figure (freshness).

3

You dodge cold-start with proprietary data

Ship company-health + comp signals from NGX/NSE filings and CAC before the crowdsourcing flywheel turns.

4

The flywheel seeds — Kenya first

Use Kenya's greater pay-openness to prove contribution + verification, then export the playbook to Nigeria with trust scaffolding already working.

5

You build a moat that survives copying

Proprietary comp + your own transition graph + verified reviews — defensible against commodity aggregators and a ROAM/MySalaryScale salary bolt-on.

⚠️ The single biggest danger is #1 and #5 together: an open lane that monetises slowly on the consumer side, against incumbents who could copy the salary layer. Both are answered by the same move — anchor on the proprietary, hard data you already hold, sell it to the side with budget, and let the consumer product compound the asset.
